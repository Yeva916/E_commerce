If you just want Authentication (Login/Signup): Use Google's token once to verify their email identity, discard it immediately, and use your native application JWT ("Enigma") for everything else.

If you want Authorization (Accessing Drive, Calendar, Photos, Gmail): Save Google's token (and a refresh token) in your database, because your backend needs to present that security clearance badge to Google every single time it reads or writes data to their Google account.

What Happens if a Hacker Tries to Tamper with It?
Imagine a malicious user captures their own token. They see their role is set to "user". They use an online tool to change the payload text to say "role": "admin", patch the base64 string back together, and send it to your backend to try and access your admin checkout dashboards.

Here is what happens when it hits your jwt.decode() block:

Your FastAPI backend reads the edited payload: "role": "admin".

Your server takes that edited payload text and mixes it with your secret key ("Enigma") to recalculate what the signature should be.

Your server compares its newly calculated signature against the signature string attached to the end of the hacker's token.

Boom! They don't match. Because the hacker doesn't know your secret key ("Enigma"), they couldn't calculate the correct math required to forge a matching signature for an admin payload.

Your server instantly throws a JWTError: Signature verification failed, flags the request as unsafe, and completely blocks access!
